# Design rules

This file governs the design pass. Every rule and every ban below is a checkable line. Each one gets a before and after screenshot at desktop width and phone width (Playwright, headless, in WSL).

## 1. What Araki actually does (sourced)

Sources: emilkowal.ski (motion), the ui-skills Scandinavian design skill (restraint, tokens), and research on Araki's design language. Most Araki sources are JoJo Wiki fan translations of interviews. The text of his craft book was not obtained, so the site rules in section 2 are inference from his stated method.

- Colour is decided per image, not fixed per character. He colours "by calculation"; Giorno is pink on one cover and blue on another. Fills are flat and matte inside outlines, which he compares to ukiyo-e.
- Named preferences: light blue + pink is his "golden combination" (sky and cherry blossoms). Green "gives me power". Blue beside pink "gives more power".
- Cover designer Mitsuru Kobayashi lists Araki's signature colours as yellow, green, hot pink, orange and purple ("Araki Purple"). Designer Noriko Narumi notes that JoJo skies are often yellow, green or pink.
- Backgrounds stay muted so the figure carries the colour. His exhibition background was mixed from five colours, like European brick, so that it does not overwhelm viewers.
- Anime colour system: a base palette per season, plus special scene or cut colouring only at emotional peaks.
- Poses use torsion (Roman sculpture, Bernini's Apollo and Daphne, fashion magazines). A figure that only stands is boring. A pose is "the shape that stays in your mind" and reads only when exaggerated.
- Composition: figures on crossing axes guide the gaze. The centre of a spread stays free of detail. Panels are right-angled by default and tilt only for motion. White pages and black panels give drama. The environment comes before the subject. The first page must make the reader turn.
- SFX are part of the picture. Anime director Tsuda said manga-style ゴゴゴ in a tense scene risks looking like a joke; staff placed SFX only in empty frames.
- There is no single JoJo typeface; each Part logo is custom. Narumi: logos are clean and legible, with motion through tilt. The JOJOLands logo uses Interstate + A1 Gothic; JoJolion uses a textured Shueido Mincho.
- The official JOJO Portal is a plain news site: quiet interface, loud art.
- Stand parameters debuted in Part 5 as chapter tailpieces, with six parameters clockwise. Anime eyecatches with them start from Stardust Crusaders. Clichés: the Menacing ゴゴゴ overlay and the "To Be Continued" arrow meme.
- Gold: no source ties gold to Araki's illustration colour (only Bulgari merchandise). The card's gold ring is our choice, not canon.

## 2. Rules for the site

1. The card stays loud and the page chrome below the stage goes quiet. The JoJo identity of the page is the card and the field behind the stage (rule 3), with the few marks of rule 7; the sections from "Why these grades" down carry none.
2. Colour per user: the page's one accent is the exact ground colour of the card on show (read back from the card's own SVG, so the two cannot differ), and the text on it is the card's own ink, which the card palette keeps at 4.5:1 or better on that ground. It follows the Light card or Dark card choice. Before a drop the accent is the ink colour. It is used as a fill, never as small text: the rule over each heading and the table header rule. On the field (rule 3) the same colour is the field itself, so there the filled button is the card's ink on the card's ground (in a black outline in the ink) and the wordmark's rule is the card's ink.
3. Ground: neutral with zero hue. Light #ffffff, dark #0e0e0e; every grey in the chrome has equal channels (#f4f4f4, #595959, #d4d4d4 light; #1a1a1a, #b3b3b3, #3a3a3a dark). No warm, brown or beige tone anywhere in the chrome outside the field (design_check.py measures every computed colour there). An error is bold and barred, not red. Flat fills only. Ban: glows, shadows, gradient text, any gradient but the one below. The field: behind the header and the stage (down to 24 px under the stage), a flat band in the ground colour of the card on show (the sample card's before a drop; the Light card or Dark card choice decides which), with a halftone screentone (a CSS radial-gradient of dots, 7 px apart, the page's one gradient) and six big faint ゴ (SVG paths read from the card's own SVG, no font, no request). The dots, the ゴ and the flash of the reveal are the ground moved 10%, 14% and 55% toward the tone away from the ink (white for black ink, black for white ink), so they never lower the text's contrast. The text on the field is the card's own ink and muted ink, and all of it keeps WCAG AA (4.5:1) on the ground, the dots and the ゴ (design_check.py and selftest.js measure it on every card, light and dark). The drop zone is a plain panel in the page's neutral ground. Sections after the stage stand on the neutral ground.
4. After a drop the To Be Continued arrow is shown in the drop zone for at least 1.0 s, even when the file is read faster, sliding in from the left of the zone (450 ms, its words fading in behind it); the reveal starts when it ends. A click or a key press skips the arrow and the reveal (the end state at once); under prefers-reduced-motion there is no arrow and nothing waits. Reveal, at most 2.2 s (also on an opened share link, which has no arrow). At 0 ms the field flashes (240 ms) to the card's ground colour and the accent shifts with it. The card lands (opacity 0 and scale 0.98 to 1, 200 ms, ease-out); then, from an inline copy of the card laid over the image (`#cardlive`; the image stays for screen readers): the four ゴ fade in one at a time along the cascade (150 ms on, 90 ms apart, 120 ms each); the name drops in with a shake of three held frames (560 to 910 ms); the radar fill grows from the centre of the chart (910 to 1310 ms); then the six grades pop in clockwise from the top, as in the anime's eyecatch (1310 ms on, 80 ms apart, 160 ms each, the last ends at 1870 ms). The parts are the `data-part` groups of the card's SVG; the delays and keyframes are in style.css; each part runs under 400 ms. A click or a key press during it skips to the end state at once; under prefers-reduced-motion there is no inline copy, no flash and no animation, and the end state is simply there. The card file, the downloaded SVG and the PNG are static: the PNG is drawn from the SVG string, never from the inline copy.
5. Type: Archivo Black for the wordmark and the headings, Archivo (weights 400 to 800) for body text; both SIL OFL, self-hosted in site/fonts. Overpass is gone. The wordmark gets one decisive tilt; nothing else tilts.
6. Layout: header; the stage; Why these grades (after a drop); How the card was built; footer. Sections stand 48 px apart. Before a drop the stage shows the sample card (site/sample.json, synthetic data, username "sample"), labelled "Sample", and the drop zone: from 900 px wide, two columns, the drop zone on the left and the sample card on the right, both whole in a 1280 x 720 window (the header puts the lede beside the wordmark to make room); on a phone one column, the drop zone first and the sample under it. After a drop the drop zone gives way to one line (file name, number of films read, a "Change file" button) and the stage is one column in this order: the file line, the card centred at the full width of the content column, the share bar; then "Why these grades". The page scrolls to the file line. Advanced (username, join date) is collapsed in the method section. One sculpted object, right angles, at most one diagonal.
7. JoJo marks in the chrome, only these three: the field's screentone and big faint ゴ (rule 3); ゴゴゴ beside the drop zone while a file is dragged over it (a column of three left of the zone where the page has room, a row of three above it where it has not; instant); and a To Be Continued arrow with its words in the zone from the drop for at least 1.0 s (rule 4; a flat arrow made with clip-path, in the page's ink; the zone's own text is hidden meanwhile). Ban: the Menacing overlay, the arrow anywhere else, ゴ as text. No kana, kanji or corner brackets in page text (the ゴ are paths, not text).
8. Motion: outside the reveal (rule 4) under 300 ms, ease-out for entrances. Card landing: opacity 0 and scale 0.98 to 1 in 200 ms. Buttons: scale(0.97) on :active in about 120 ms. Drag-over state is instant. Scrolling the card into view is instant. Ban: hover lifts, scroll fades, motion in the card file itself (no `<animate>`, `<set>` or keyframes in the SVG), any library, any request. The site test runs under prefers-reduced-motion (`--force-prefers-reduced-motion`), so the reveal adds no time to its 60 s virtual-time budget.
9. Anti-slop bans: emoji or icon sprinkles, gradient text, uniform rounded cards with shadows, "unleash", "seamless" and "powered by AI" copy, a centred hero with three feature cards. Copy uses real numbers (9,787 films, 34,523 users). The ★ in a data line is the Letterboxd rating unit, not an icon.
10. Share bar, under the card: Download PNG is the one filled button, then Copy image (the PNG on the clipboard), Share (the Web Share API with the PNG file; hidden where the API cannot share files) and Copy link. Download SVG and Download stats (JSON) are text links in the method section.
11. Share link: Copy link writes `#c=<base64url>` after the page's address: the data the card needs (name, username, type, six grades with asterisks and chart steps, ability name and sentence, nearest canon, accent), about 400 characters, built by `share.rs` and checked on decoding. Next to the button the page says the link contains the username and grades. Opening such a link renders the card from the fragment alone (a fragment is never sent to a server); the "Why these grades" section is not shown, since the link carries no stats. A malformed fragment shows the normal page. Tests: round trip and malformed cases in share.rs, tests/share.rs, the wasm lib and selftest.js.
12. "Why these grades": for each stat its value, its film, what it was graded on and its status; then the ability and the weakness, why the Stand has its name, the signature film and the archetype note. The data credits are in the footer. These are the items the card no longer draws (section 4).
13. Card theme: the options are "Light card" and "Dark card"; the select is shown only after a drop (or on an opened link). The card starts in the system's theme and follows it until the user picks one.
14. Requests: the page loads its own files only (including sample.json); 0 requests after a drop, over the whole reveal, and when a share link is opened. The reveal, the field and the drop-zone marks use CSS and the page's own JavaScript only.
15. Every rule and every ban in this section gets a before and after screenshot at desktop width and phone width.

## 3. Card-level fixes

- Why line: give it its own row near the signature film, at body size. It is now about 11 px, squeezed between the name and 本体.
- PROVISIONAL DATA badge: it is red like the grades. Make it neutral or drop it; the footer asterisk already covers it.
- Type mix: serif only for the Stand name and the 【】 labels. The card's sans for evidence lines and the ability box.
- Footer: about 9 px is unreadable on a phone PNG. Keep one credit line on the card and move the TMDB disclaimer to the site.
- Glyphs: 40 artist credits draw boxes because the card font subset lacks their glyphs (mostly Japanese, Korean and Chinese names, plus a few Latin Extended letters such as ć and ş). Add those glyphs to the subset. Do not fold or drop the names.
- Page chrome: Download PNG is the one filled button. SVG and JSON are quiet text buttons with the same height and radius. Remove the bordered container around the card (double frame).
- Grade C legend: the card says 人間並, JoJo Wiki gives 人間と同じ. Check a Part 5 volume before the AAT.

## 4. Card palette, content and lettering pass

Second pass, from the reference covers and pages in local/ref (git-ignored; never copied into site/, docs/ or any committed path). It replaces the muted brick ground of the first pass: Araki's brick-mixed background was an exhibition choice, the covers and Stand pages are flat saturated colour. The third pass (rules 5, 6 and 8 to 15) cuts the card down to what a phone can read. Where it conflicts with section 3 (why line, PROVISIONAL DATA pill, footer, legend), this section wins.

1. The card takes the same per-user palette as the page: the key comes from the signature stat (site/accent.js mapping), and each key has its cover colour (Power hot pink, Speed yellow, Range blue, Stamina green, Precision purple, Potential orange).
2. Ground: one flat saturated colour sampled from the covers, per key and per card theme. Never brown, brick or near-black. Every ground and radar fill is a bin colour that `tools/ref_palette.py` sampled from a named cover; `docs/ref-palette.json` lists the image of each, and a test in palette.rs checks the table against it.
3. Radar fill, grades and ゴ: a second sampled cover colour (the complement of the ground). Other text is the ink of the ground (black or white, whichever reads better).
4. Flat fills only. Black outlines are the manga's and are allowed (a stroked black path under the fill path); gradients, glows, shadows, patterns and opacity stay banned.
5. The Stand name follows the anime eyecatch (local/ref/iuhipmy89x781.png, Star Platinum): level, no tilt, in Noto Serif JP Bold with no outline and no shadow, at the top left, with 【STAND NAME】 above it in the same serif, smaller, in capitals. The slant belongs to series logos (rule 5 of section 2 keeps it for the page's wordmark) and to the manga's sound effects, not to a Stand name: the only rotated things on the card are the ゴ of the cascade (rule 13). The name is as large as its line allows, up to 104 px; no name in names.json clips: every one fits uncut at 40 px or more (test `every_name_in_names_json_fits_the_header_uncut`). The name is drawn in the card's ink (white on the dark grounds and the purple, black on the bright ones): pure white, with no outline behind it, is under 3:1 on the yellow, green, blue, orange and teal light grounds, and rule 7 holds.
6. Chart: the Part 5 stat chart: a hexagon with a ring per grade, three spokes, the radar fill, the grade on each vertex (an asterisk after a provisional grade; see rule 16) and the Japanese label beside it (破壊力 top, スピード upper right, 射程距離 lower right, 持続力 bottom, 精密動作性 lower left, 成長性 upper left). No scale dots on the fill, no A to E ticks, no legend.
7. Contrast (WCAG AA, `tools/site_test/card_contrast.py` on the real SVGs, 6 accents and none, light and dark): text on the ground (the Stand name, the two labels and the user included) and on the paper 4.5:1; the grades and the ゴ, large lettering, 3:1 against their outline.
8. Content: the card keeps only the Stand name under 【STAND NAME】, the Stand master (【STAND MASTER】 and the user name, bottom right), the type line in Japanese and English, the hexagon with six grades and asterisks, the ability name with one sentence of at most 20 words, the nearest canon line, and one footer line, drawn in two rows at the bottom left: "standuser.pages.dev · Fan work, not affiliated · * provisional". The nearest canon line lists as many of the three names as fit, never cutting a name.
9. Removed from the card: the stat value lines, film titles, graded-on lines, weakness, signature film, why line, archetype caveat, PROVISIONAL DATA pill and data credit line. They are not lost: the analysis JSON the wasm returns still carries every one in `analysis.card` (`evidence`, `coverage`, `weakness`, `signature`, `why`, `type_note`, `badge`, `credit`), and a test in standuser-wasm checks it. The page does not show them yet.
10. Space: the hexagon plate is 600 px of the 1080 px card (55.5%; a test requires 55% or more). The Stand name goes up to 104 px; the median name in names.json is drawn at 104 px, the longest (28 letters) at 49 px.
11. Fonts, all SIL OFL, embedded as outlines (licence texts in crates/standuser-core/data; `tools/make_card_fonts.py`, `tools/make_fonts.py`): Archivo Black for the Latin display text (the ability name; the grades, with the black outline); Archivo at weight 700 for all other Latin text; Dela Gothic One with the black outline for Japanese at 48 px or larger (the ゴ, and the 完成 grade); Noto Sans JP Bold, no outline, for Japanese below 48 px (the type, the stat labels, 完成); Noto Serif JP Bold, no outline, for the Stand name, 【STAND NAME】, 【STAND MASTER】 and the user name (`tools/make_serif_font.py`, subset to every character in names.json that the font has, the labels and Latin-1). No other font. A character Archivo lacks in user-supplied text (a Cyrillic or Korean director name) falls back to Noto Sans JP Bold; so does a character the serif lacks in a user name. A test (`every_text_is_in_the_face_its_role_names`) checks the role of every piece of text.
12. No text on the card is below 34 px in card units (`MIN_TEXT`), so it is about 12 px at the 390 px phone width. A test checks every run of every card, long names and long user names included. Because the sentence is shown at 34 px, it is a short line made for the card (`ability::card_line`, one figure each), not the long ability text (that stays in `card.ability`); it has no ★, which Archivo lacks.
13. ゴゴゴ: one diagonal cascade of four ゴ, as in local/ref/004385f76965c1727c35e5134bc5fe89.jpg, at 36 degrees from horizontal (a test requires 35 to 45, for the whole cascade and for each step): from under the Stand name at the top left down to the right, in the gap between the name block and the hexagon's upper left, each smaller than the one before (60, 54, 48, 42 px), in Dela Gothic One in the second colour with the black outline, each leaning 8 degrees about its centre (the card's only rotation). Clear of all text by 10 px, of the hexagon's plate by 10 px (polygon test, not its bounding box) and of the frame. Four is what the gap holds at that angle with glyphs of 40 px or more: a line down to the right from the top left meets the hexagon's upper-left edge after about 300 px, and the 成長性 label stands under it (six at 96 to 48 px fitted only in the steep strip beside the hexagon, at about 80 degrees). Test `the_gogogo_are_one_shallow_cascade_in_the_gap_clear_of_all_text` checks 14 grounds (6 accents and none, light and dark) with the longest name in names.json (YORKTOWN AND THE RETURN HOME), names with deep descenders at 104 px, a one-letter name and the widest type lines.
14. Frame: one frame, the thick black border (16 px), flush with the card edge. No outer margin, no thin inner line. All text stays inside it.
15. Layout: the header is as in the eyecatch: 【STAND NAME】 and the name at the top left, on a fixed baseline, the label set just over the name's tallest letter, the ink of its bracket on the margin; the type in Japanese and English at the right under the name. The Stand master is the bottom right corner: 【STAND MASTER】 over the user name, right-aligned to the margin. The bottom is laid out from the foot up: the footer (two rows, bottom left, beside the master), the nearest canon line, the sentence, the ability name. Tests check that no two pieces of text touch and that the label sits over the name.
16. Grades: the six grade letters are the largest lettering on the card after the Stand name (a long name is drawn smaller than a grade, down to 49 px): Archivo Black at 80 px (the 完成 grade, Dela Gothic One, at 72 px), in the second colour with the black outline, centred on their hexagon vertex, with the asterisk after a provisional grade. They are not circled: a circle round a 72 px letter does not fit between the type line and the ability block. Each vertex's Japanese label stands beside the grade (top and bottom) or above or below it (the four side vertices). A test (`the_grades_are_the_largest_lettering_after_the_stand_name`) checks size, face, outline and that every other run is smaller; card_contrast.py checks each against its outline at 3:1.
