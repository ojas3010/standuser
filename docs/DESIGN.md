# Design rules

This file governs the design pass. Every rule and every ban below is a checkable line. Each one gets a before and after screenshot at desktop width and phone width (Playwright, headless, in WSL).

## 1. What Araki actually does (sourced)

Sources: emilkowal.ski (motion), the ui-skills Scandinavian design skill (restraint, tokens), and research on Araki's design language. Most Araki sources are JoJo Wiki fan translations of interviews. The text of his craft book was not obtained, so the site rules in section 2 are inference from his stated method.

- Colour is decided per image, not fixed per character. He colours "by calculation"; Giorno is pink on one cover and blue on another. Fills are flat and matte inside outlines, which he compares to ukiyo-e.
- Named preferences: light blue + pink is his "golden combination" (sky and cherry blossoms). Green "gives me power". Blue beside pink "gives more power".
- Cover designer Mitsuru Kobayashi lists Araki's signature colours as yellow, green, hot pink, orange and purple ("Araki Purple"). Designer Noriko Narumi notes that JoJo skies are often yellow, green or pink.
- Backgrounds stay muted so the figure carries the colour. His exhibition background was mixed from five colours, like European brick, so that it does not overwhelm viewers.
- Anime colour system: a base palette per season, plus special scene or cut colouring only at emotional peaks.
- Poses use torsion (Roman sculpture, Bernini's Apollo and Daphne, fashion magazines). A figure that only stands is boring. A pose is "the shape that stays in your mind" and reads only when exaggerated.
- Composition: figures on crossing axes guide the gaze. The centre of a spread stays free of detail. Panels are right-angled by default and tilt only for motion. White pages and black panels give drama. The environment comes before the subject. The first page must make the reader turn.
- SFX are part of the picture. Anime director Tsuda said manga-style ゴゴゴ in a tense scene risks looking like a joke; staff placed SFX only in empty frames.
- There is no single JoJo typeface; each Part logo is custom. Narumi: logos are clean and legible, with motion through tilt. The JOJOLands logo uses Interstate + A1 Gothic; JoJolion uses a textured Shueido Mincho.
- The official JOJO Portal is a plain news site: quiet interface, loud art.
- Stand parameters debuted in Part 5 as chapter tailpieces, with six parameters clockwise. Anime eyecatches with them start from Stardust Crusaders. Clichés: the Menacing ゴゴゴ overlay and the "To Be Continued" arrow meme.
- Gold: no source ties gold to Araki's illustration colour (only Bulgari merchandise). The card's gold ring is our choice, not canon.

## 2. Rules for the site

1. The card stays loud and the page chrome goes quiet. Only the card carries JoJo identity.
2. Colour per user: the page's one accent is the exact ground colour of the card on show (read back from the card's own SVG, so the two cannot differ), and the text on it is the card's own ink, which the card palette keeps at 4.5:1 or better on that ground. It follows the Light card or Dark card choice. Before a drop the accent is the ink colour. It is used as a fill, never as small text: the filled button (with a black outline), the rule under the wordmark, the rule over each heading, the table header rule.
3. Ground: neutral with zero hue. Light #ffffff, dark #0e0e0e; every grey in the chrome has equal channels (#f4f4f4, #595959, #d4d4d4 light; #1a1a1a, #b3b3b3, #3a3a3a dark). No warm, brown or beige tone anywhere in the chrome before a drop (design_check.py measures every computed colour). An error is bold and barred, not red. Flat fills only. Ban: gradients, glows, shadows.
4. One colour event at the reveal: the card lands, holds, then one palette shift (the accent from ink to the card's ground). It is off under prefers-reduced-motion.
5. Type: Archivo Black for the wordmark and the headings, Archivo (weights 400 to 800) for body text; both SIL OFL, self-hosted in site/fonts. Overpass is gone. The wordmark gets one decisive tilt; nothing else tilts.
6. Layout order: header; the card; the drop zone. Before a drop the card is a sample made from synthetic data (site/sample.json), labelled "Sample", above the drop zone. After a drop the drop zone gives way to one line (file name, number of films read, a "Change file" button) and the card scrolls into view. Then, in this order: the share bar, "Why these grades", "How the card was built". One sculpted object, right angles, at most one diagonal. Single column on mobile.
7. Ban in the chrome: ゴゴゴ (card only), the To Be Continued arrow, the Menacing overlay. No kana, kanji or corner brackets in page text.
8. Motion: under 300 ms, ease-out for entrances. Card reveal: opacity 0 and scale 0.98 to 1 in about 200 ms. Buttons: scale(0.97) on :active in about 120 ms. Drag-over state is instant. Scrolling the card into view is instant. Ban: hover lifts, scroll fades, animated radar.
9. Anti-slop bans: emoji or icon sprinkles, gradient text, uniform rounded cards with shadows, "unleash", "seamless" and "powered by AI" copy, a centred hero with three feature cards. Copy uses real numbers (9,787 films, 34,523 users). The ★ in a data line is the Letterboxd rating unit, not an icon.
10. Share bar, under the card: Download PNG is the one filled button, then Copy image (the PNG on the clipboard), Share (the Web Share API with the PNG file; hidden where the API cannot share files) and Copy link. Download SVG and Download stats (JSON) are text links in the method section.
11. Share link: Copy link writes `#c=<base64url>` after the page's address: the data the card needs (name, username, type, six grades with asterisks and chart steps, ability name and sentence, nearest canon, accent), about 400 characters, built by `share.rs` and checked on decoding. Next to the button the page says the link contains the username and grades. Opening such a link renders the card from the fragment alone (a fragment is never sent to a server); the "Why these grades" section is not shown, since the link carries no stats. A malformed fragment shows the normal page. Tests: round trip and malformed cases in share.rs, tests/share.rs, the wasm lib and selftest.js.
12. "Why these grades": for each stat its value, its film, what it was graded on and its status; then the ability and the weakness, why the Stand has its name, the signature film, the archetype note and the data credits. These are the items the card no longer draws (section 4).
13. Card theme: the options are "Light card" and "Dark card"; the card starts in the system's theme and follows it until the user picks one.
14. Requests: the page loads its own files only (including sample.json); 0 requests after a drop and when a share link is opened.
15. Every rule and every ban in this section gets a before and after screenshot at desktop width and phone width.

## 3. Card-level fixes

- Why line: give it its own row near the signature film, at body size. It is now about 11 px, squeezed between the name and 本体.
- PROVISIONAL DATA badge: it is red like the grades. Make it neutral or drop it; the footer asterisk already covers it.
- Type mix: serif only for the Stand name and the 【】 labels. The card's sans for evidence lines and the ability box.
- Footer: about 9 px is unreadable on a phone PNG. Keep one credit line on the card and move the TMDB disclaimer to the site.
- Glyphs: 40 artist credits draw boxes because the card font subset lacks their glyphs (mostly Japanese, Korean and Chinese names, plus a few Latin Extended letters such as ć and ş). Add those glyphs to the subset. Do not fold or drop the names.
- Page chrome: Download PNG is the one filled button. SVG and JSON are quiet text buttons with the same height and radius. Remove the bordered container around the card (double frame).
- Grade C legend: the card says 人間並, JoJo Wiki gives 人間と同じ. Check a Part 5 volume before the AAT.

## 4. Card palette, content and lettering pass

Second pass, from the reference covers and pages in local/ref (git-ignored; never copied into site/, docs/ or any committed path). It replaces the muted brick ground of the first pass: Araki's brick-mixed background was an exhibition choice, the covers and Stand pages are flat saturated colour. The third pass (rules 5, 6 and 8 to 15) cuts the card down to what a phone can read. Where it conflicts with section 3 (why line, PROVISIONAL DATA pill, footer, legend), this section wins.

1. The card takes the same per-user palette as the page: the key comes from the signature stat (site/accent.js mapping), and each key has its cover colour (Power hot pink, Speed yellow, Range blue, Stamina green, Precision purple, Potential orange).
2. Ground: one flat saturated colour sampled from the covers, per key and per card theme. Never brown, brick or near-black. Every ground and radar fill is a bin colour that `tools/ref_palette.py` sampled from a named cover; `docs/ref-palette.json` lists the image of each, and a test in palette.rs checks the table against it.
3. Radar fill, grades and ゴ: a second sampled cover colour (the complement of the ground). Other text is the ink of the ground (black or white, whichever reads better).
4. Flat fills only. Black outlines are the manga's and are allowed (a stroked black path under the fill path); gradients, glows, shadows, patterns and opacity stay banned.
5. The Stand name takes one tilt of -8 degrees, like a Part logo. It is the card's one rotated element. No name in names.json clips: every one fits uncut at 34 px or more (test `every_name_in_names_json_fits_the_tilted_header_uncut`).
6. Chart: the Part 5 stat chart: a hexagon with a ring per grade, three spokes, the radar fill, the grade on each vertex (an asterisk after a provisional grade; see rule 16) and the Japanese label beside it (破壊力 top, スピード upper right, 射程距離 lower right, 持続力 bottom, 精密動作性 lower left, 成長性 upper left). No scale dots on the fill, no A to E ticks, no legend.
7. Contrast (WCAG AA, `tools/site_test/card_contrast.py` on the real SVGs, 6 accents and none, light and dark): text on the ground and on the paper 4.5:1; the outlined Stand name against its black outline 4.5:1; the grades and the ゴ, large lettering, 3:1 against their outline.
8. Content: the card keeps only the Stand name, the Stand master (本体―), the type line in Japanese and English, the hexagon with six grades and asterisks, the ability name with one sentence of at most 20 words, the nearest canon line, and one footer line: "standuser.pages.dev · Fan work, not affiliated · * provisional". The nearest canon line lists as many of the three names as fit, never cutting a name.
9. Removed from the card: the stat value lines, film titles, graded-on lines, weakness, signature film, why line, archetype caveat, PROVISIONAL DATA pill and data credit line. They are not lost: the analysis JSON the wasm returns still carries every one in `analysis.card` (`evidence`, `coverage`, `weakness`, `signature`, `why`, `type_note`, `badge`, `credit`), and a test in standuser-wasm checks it. The page does not show them yet.
10. Space: the hexagon plate is 600 px of the 1080 px card (55.5%; a test requires 55% or more). The Stand name goes up to 110 px (it was 80); the median name in names.json is now drawn at 89 px, the longest at 43 px.
11. Fonts, all SIL OFL, embedded as outlines (licence texts in crates/standuser-core/data; `tools/make_card_fonts.py`, `tools/make_fonts.py`): Archivo Black for the Stand name and the other Latin display text (ability name; the grades, with the black outline); Archivo at weight 700 for all other Latin text; Dela Gothic One with the black outline for Japanese at 48 px or larger (the ゴ, and the 完成 grade); Noto Sans JP Bold, no outline, for Japanese below 48 px (本体, the type, the stat labels, 完成). No other font. A character Archivo lacks in user-supplied text (a Cyrillic or Korean director name) falls back to Noto Sans JP Bold. A test (`every_text_is_in_the_face_its_role_names`) checks the role of every piece of text.
12. No text on the card is below 34 px in card units (`MIN_TEXT`), so it is about 12 px at the 390 px phone width. A test checks every run of every card, long names and long user names included. Because the sentence is shown at 34 px, it is a short line made for the card (`ability::card_line`, one figure each), not the long ability text (that stays in `card.ability`); it has no ★, which Archivo lacks.
13. ゴ: three, in Dela Gothic One, in the second colour with the black outline, on a diagonal down the left side between the card edge and the hexagon, staggered, in three sizes (66, 56, 48 px, all smaller than the grades), clear of all text and of the hexagon (test `the_gogogo_are_three_staggered_on_a_diagonal_clear_of_all_text`).
14. Frame: one frame, the thick black border (16 px), flush with the card edge. No outer margin, no thin inner line. All text stays inside it.
15. Layout: the Stand master sits in the free corner above the name's low left end and the type in the corner under its high right end (the name's bottom edge is one fixed line, so those corners are always free); the bottom is laid out from the foot up: footer, nearest canon, the sentence, the ability name. Tests check that no two pieces of text touch and that the name clears them all.
16. Grades: the six grade letters are the largest lettering on the card after the Stand name: Archivo Black at 80 px (the 完成 grade, Dela Gothic One, at 72 px), in the second colour with the black outline, centred on their hexagon vertex, with the asterisk after a provisional grade. They are not circled: a circle round a 72 px letter does not fit between the type line and the ability block. Each vertex's Japanese label stands beside the grade (top and bottom) or above or below it (the four side vertices). A test (`the_grades_are_the_largest_lettering_after_the_stand_name`) checks size, face, outline and that every other run is smaller; card_contrast.py checks each against its outline at 3:1.
